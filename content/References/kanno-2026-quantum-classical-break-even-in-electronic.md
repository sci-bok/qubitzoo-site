## Abstract
Here we demonstrate quantum--classical break-even for chemical dynamics applications, reaching useful accuracy with a quantum-hardware wall-clock time comparable to that estimated for practical classical computing resources. To this end, we develop a workflow for problems that are hardware-native but classically hard. We target large ring-shaped molecules that map naturally onto quantum hardware with neighboring connections. Exploiting the spatial locality of interactions, we compress short-time evolution within a small region using tensor networks. We then use rotational symmetry to replicate the compressed circuit in space and time, enabling long-time dynamics of large systems with practical classical processing times. We apply the workflow to conjugated macrocycles with rotational periodicity, 12-qubit benzene and 120-qubit [60]annulene, by embedding their electronic structure onto loops of IBM quantum processors. For the annulene, we compared the quantum-processing-unit (QPU) wall-clock times for hole-doped nonequilibrium dynamics to the estimated wall-clock time of two state-of-the-art classical methods, Majorana propagation and matrix-product-state time evolution based on the time-dependent variational principle. We show a situation in which a hybrid quantum--classical strategy is comparable to a classical-only counterpart in terms of both accuracy and computational time for a conventional high-performance computing system under ideal parallelization. These results show that quantum hardware can become a realistic computational option for chemical simulations for practitioners in chemical science and engineering.
