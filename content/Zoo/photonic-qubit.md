## Description

A **photonic qubit** stores quantum information in optical modes rather than stationary matter degrees of freedom. The central advantage is that photons already behave like the communication layer quantum hardware wants: they propagate with very low decoherence, interface naturally with fiber and integrated photonics, and can connect distant modules without an explicit transducer.

That same advantage creates the platform's core constraint. Photons are excellent carriers but weakly interacting processors. In practice, photonic quantum computing therefore splits into two separate design questions that should not be conflated:

- **Encoding question:** which optical degree of freedom holds the qubit, for example spatial modes in [[dual-rail-photonic-qubit]] or temporal modes in [[time-bin-photonic-qubit]]?
- **Architecture question:** how do we manufacture entangling power and fault tolerance despite weak native interactions, for example with [[linear-optical-photonic-qubit]], [[photonic-cluster-state-mbqc-qubit]], or [[fusion-based-photonic-qubit]]?

This umbrella note is the entry point when the question is simply why photons are attractive at all, before choosing a specific encoding or computation model.

## Formalism and control

A discrete-variable photonic qubit occupies a two-dimensional subspace of optical modes. For two orthogonal modes with creation operators $a_0^\dagger$ and $a_1^\dagger$,

$$
|0_L\rangle=a_0^\dagger|\mathrm{vac}\rangle,\qquad
|1_L\rangle=a_1^\dagger|\mathrm{vac}\rangle,
$$

so an arbitrary qubit is $|\psi\rangle=(\alpha a_0^\dagger+\beta a_1^\dagger)|\mathrm{vac}\rangle$. The modes may be polarization, path, time, or frequency bins; the logical algebra is the same even though the hardware used to stabilize and measure the modes differs.

Lossless linear optics is generated by a number-preserving bilinear Hamiltonian,

$$
H=\hbar\sum_{jk} h_{jk}a_j^\dagger a_k,
$$

which implements a unitary mode transformation $a_j^\dagger\mapsto\sum_k U_{kj}a_k^\dagger$. Beam splitters, phase shifters, waveplates, and electro-optic modulators therefore provide deterministic single-qubit rotations. Passive linear optics does not supply a deterministic photon-photon interaction; entangling operations require measurement, ancilla photons and feed-forward, pre-built resource states, or a physical optical nonlinearity.

Photon loss maps the one-photon codespace into vacuum. When the absence is detected, the error is naturally an erasure; finite detector efficiency, multiphoton contamination, mode mismatch, and unheralded loss determine how cleanly that favorable model holds in practice.

## Motivation

- **Best native flying qubit:** photons are the cleanest hardware for long-distance transport and chip-to-chip interconnects.
- **Low transport decoherence:** room-temperature optics and fiber compatibility make distributed architectures natural.
- **Manufacturing leverage:** integrated photonics, detectors, and multiplexing infrastructure can be borrowed from mature optical engineering stacks.
- **Architectural flexibility:** the same family supports communication-first nodes, gate-based LOQC, cluster-state MBQC, and fusion-based scaling strategies.

## Experimental status

Single-photon polarization states were already used as a two-dimensional information carrier in the 1984 BB84 protocol. Reck et al. showed in 1994 how arbitrary finite-dimensional optical unitaries can be decomposed into beam-splitter transformations, and Knill, Laflamme, and Milburn showed in 2001 that scalable computation is possible in principle using single photons, linear optics, measurement, ancillas, and feed-forward.

Photonic qubits are mature for quantum communication and have supported integrated single- and two-qubit gate demonstrations. This does not make a fault-tolerant photonic computer mature: system performance depends on the joint efficiency and indistinguishability of sources, switches, interferometers, detectors, multiplexing, feed-forward, and loss-tolerant error correction.

## Scaling bottlenecks

- End-to-end optical efficiency multiplies source, coupling, switching, propagation, and detector efficiencies; individually strong components can still produce a poor system yield.
- Two-photon interference requires photons to be indistinguishable in spectrum, time, polarization, and spatial mode.
- Linear-optical entangling operations are measurement-induced and therefore consume ancillas, resource states, multiplexing, and fast feed-forward.
- Loss is advantageous only when it is reliably flagged; missed photons and multiphoton source events create unheralded logical errors.

## Key questions

- Which encoding best matches the hardware path: [[dual-rail-photonic-qubit]] or [[time-bin-photonic-qubit]]?
- Is the architecture fundamentally gate-based ([[linear-optical-photonic-qubit]]) or resource-state-based ([[photonic-cluster-state-mbqc-qubit]], [[fusion-based-photonic-qubit]])?
- Does the dominant noise stay structured enough to be treated as erasure-like loss rather than generic hidden Pauli error?
