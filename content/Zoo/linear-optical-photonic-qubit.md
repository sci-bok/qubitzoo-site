## Description

The **linear-optical photonic qubit** is the gate-based linear-optics quantum-computing (LOQC) layer of the broader photonic-qubit family. Quantum information is usually encoded into single-photon degrees of freedom such as **dual-rail/path** or **polarization** modes, while state preparation, single-qubit control, and measurement are performed with passive interferometers, single-photon sources, and photodetectors.

A canonical dual-rail encoding is

$$
|0_L\rangle = |1\rangle_a|0\rangle_b, \qquad |1_L\rangle = |0\rangle_a|1\rangle_b.
$$

Beam splitters and phase shifters then implement deterministic single-qubit unitaries inside that logical subspace. The central challenge is two-qubit logic: passive linear optics does **not** provide a native deterministic photon-photon interaction, so entangling gates are built from interference, ancilla photons, measurement, and classical feed-forward.

The foundational Knill, Laflamme, and Milburn result (KLM, 2001) showed that this is still enough for scalable universal quantum computation in principle. In that sense, LOQC is historically important not because its primitive entangling gates are easy, but because it proved that measurement-induced effective nonlinearities can replace direct microscopic interactions.

## Hamiltonian

Passive LOQC control is well represented by a number-conserving bilinear bosonic Hamiltonian

$$
H_{\mathrm{passive}} = \sum_{ij} h_{ij} a_i^\dagger a_j,
$$

with Hermitian mode-coupling matrix $h$. For a two-mode logical qubit, a beam splitter is generated by

$$
H_{\mathrm{BS}} = \kappa a^\dagger b + \kappa^* b^\dagger a,
$$

while a phase shifter on one mode is represented by

$$
H_\phi = \phi\, a^\dagger a.
$$

These operations generate the usual $SU(2)$ single-qubit control on the encoded subspace. What they do **not** supply is a native two-qubit entangling Hamiltonian. In LOQC, the nontrivial multi-qubit resource is instead **measurement-induced effective nonlinearity**: ancilla-state preparation, multi-photon interference, photon counting, and feed-forward produce heralded entangling operations or larger resource states.

## Motivation

- **Minimal transport decoherence:** photons propagate cleanly through waveguides and fiber, making LOQC naturally network-compatible.
- **Deterministic single-qubit control:** passive interferometers implement high-quality logical rotations without a nonlinear medium.
- **Scalability proof by measurement:** KLM established that universal QC is possible without direct photon-photon interactions.
- **Erasure-friendly failure mode:** photon loss often leaves the logical subspace in a flagged way rather than as an arbitrary unknown rotation.
- **Architectural bridge:** modern photonic cluster-state and fusion-based schemes can be viewed as descendants of the same measurement-and-feed-forward logic, optimized for better fault-tolerant overheads.

## Experimental Status

**Scalable linear-optical route established, KLM (2001):**
- Showed that universal quantum computation is possible with single-photon sources, passive linear optics, photon counting, and feed-forward.
- Made explicit the core LOQC trade: easy transport and control, expensive entangling logic.

**First landmark all-optical two-qubit logic experiment, O'Brien et al. (2003):**
- Demonstrated a post-selected all-optical controlled-NOT gate.
- Confirmed that nontrivial photonic two-qubit logic is experimentally reachable without a direct optical nonlinearity.

**Integrated and reconfigurable linear-optics era (2000s-2020s):**
- Mach-Zehnder meshes, low-loss waveguides, and improved single-photon detectors made complex interferometric circuits practical.
- The main hardware constraints remain loss, source purity, indistinguishability, detector efficiency, and feed-forward latency.

**2024-2026 direction check:**
- A targeted audit search did not find a peer-reviewed 2025-2026 gate-based LOQC result that displaces KLM or O'Brien as the canonical umbrella milestones for this entry.
- The strongest recent momentum is architectural rather than gate-demo-centric, with modern photonic fault-tolerance increasingly expressed through cluster-state and fusion-based resource-state consumption rather than literal inline KLM gate teleportation.
